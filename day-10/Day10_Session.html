    Ask the roomIf you could only keep one layer when a deadline is tight, which would you keep, and what would you lose?
    Expected: usually the rules plus the gate (cheap, exact, automatic). What you lose: meaning problems no word list can catch, like faithful-but-wrong answers, subtle invented policy and tone. That trade-off is exactly what choosing five metrics is about.

    Part 1 · Context · One last real case

    Measuring volume isn’t measuring quality

    Klarna · 2024–2025

    “The work of 700 agents”, then rehiring humans

    In February 2024, the payments company said its AI assistant was handling about three-quarters of customer chats, about 2.3 million conversations a month, the work of 700 full-time agents.

    By May 2025, its CEO said the focus on cost had gone too far, quality had suffered, and Klarna started hiring human agents again: AI for routine questions, people for the rest. It still credits the AI with large savings.

    What that means for your capstone

    Volume, speed and cost were measured. Quality for the hard cases was not measured as carefully.

    A release decision isn’t only “ship or not”. It can be CONDITIONAL: ship for routine questions, route complaints, data and money to a person.

    Your five metrics decide what quality means for your agent.

    Ask the roomIf you had been Klarna’s QA lead in 2024, which one metric would you have insisted on reporting next to “2.3 million chats”?
    Expected (examples): resolution quality on hard cases (complaints, disputes), measured against expected outcomes; human-escalation rate and why; customer satisfaction by case type, not on average; a rubric for empathy and correctness, calibrated against human reviewers. Anything that measures quality per risk area, not just volume.

    Part 1 · Context · Selecting 5 metrics for the agent

    Five metrics: one job each, every one defensible

    1. Start from your risks, not from the tool’s menu. Each metric covers at least one numbered risk.
    2. Mix kinds: at least one exact check (rules, canary), at least one judge check.
    3. Cover both RAG layers if your agent retrieves: one for retrieval, one for the answer.
    4. Justify the pass mark: calibration, a case you saw, or the risk level. Never “0.7 because that’s the default”.
    5. Say what you dropped, and why.

    Lane example (project/metrics.md):

    rulesrisks 1, 2, 4exact, the canary
    pii leakagerisk 20.9
    context recallrisk 30.7
    Answer correctnessrisk 30.7
    Resists manipulationrisk 40.7

    Dropped: toxicity, since tone is covered by rules and the empathy rubric. Other checks can stay on as supporting; these five are the ones you defend.

    Ask the roomA colleague picks toxicity, bias, hallucination, faithfulness and answer relevancy for a payments assistant. What’s missing?
    Expected: nothing exact for the biggest payments risks: asking for OTP/PIN, wrong-account refunds, data leaks. No rules, no canary, no injection check, and no retrieval check if it uses documents. Five judge metrics, all generic; none chosen from that app’s risks.

    Part 1 · Context · Running evals and reporting findings

    A findings report a manager reads in one minute

    python kit.py report writes it

    1 · Decision and the evidence in one line
    2 · The five metrics: pass marks, results, why
    3 · Results by risk area
    4 · Top defects, each typed app / judge / test / policy
    5 · Failing cases, with reasons
    6 · Gaps and the next measurement

    Present it in 3 minutes

    30s Decision
    60s Five metrics and why
    60s Top defects, with evidence
    30s Gaps and next step

    Lead with the decision. Nobody needs the method before they know the answer.

    Ask the roomYour pass rate is 91% and the gate failed on one personal-data case. What’s your first sentence to the product owner?
    Expected (something like): “Not ready to release: one case leaked another customer’s details, which blocks release regardless of the 91%. Everything else is close; here’s the fix and the rerun date.” Decision first, the blocking reason, then the path forward.

    Part 1 · Context · Capstone peer review

    Review the evidence, not the person

    Five questions (project/peer_review.md)

    1 · Do the 5 metrics cover the biggest risks?
    2 · Is the dataset trustworthy?
    3 · Are thresholds justified, not guessed?
    4 · Does the decision follow from the evidence?
    5 · Could a developer act on the top defects tomorrow?

    Score 1–3, each with one sentence of evidence. Then: the strongest thing, one change, and would you sign it off?

    Good review comments

    Specific: “L12 failed on PII but it isn’t in your top defects.”
    Kind: “Your trap case R01 is excellent; I’d add one for payments too.”
    Actionable: “Raise context recall’s pass mark only after calibrating it.”

    Ask the roomA reviewer writes: “Looks good, nice work.” Why is that a weak review, and what would make it useful?
    Expected: it gives the author nothing to act on and no evidence it was read. A useful review names a specific case, metric or decision, says what’s strong and what to change, and states whether the reviewer would sign off, and why.

    Part 2 · Capstone · 30 minutes · on your own

    Choose, run, decide, report

    1project/metrics.md: your 5 headline metrics, each linked to a risk number, with a pass mark, a reason, and how you know it works. Plus one you dropped.
    2project/project.ini: make sure all 5 are on with those pass marks
    3python kit.py run --ci
    4Changed a rubric? python kit.py calibrate
    5project/verdict.md: decision, evidence, top 3 defects with real case IDs
    6python kit.py evidence, then python kit.py report
    7python kit.py review: aim for stage 10 ★

    At the end you need one file open: project/findings.md (Ctrl + Shift + V to preview). You’ll present from it.

    Not everything needs to pass. A BLOCK with clear evidence is a perfectly good capstone. An unjustified RELEASE isn’t.
