    Part 1 · Context · Real incidents

    Looking things up doesn’t stop wrong answers

    Google · May 2024

    AI Overviews: glue on pizza

    Google’s new AI answers at the top of search told a user to add glue to pizza sauce to stop the cheese sliding off, and another that eating a small rock a day is healthy.

    Both came from real web pages the system had retrieved: an old joke on a forum, and an article from a satirical news site. The answers were faithful to their sources. The sources were the problem.

    Failure in retrieval: the wrong documents were fetched and trusted.

    Stanford RegLab · 2024

    “Hallucination-free” legal research

    Legal research tools from LexisNexis and Thomson Reuters use RAG, and were marketed as avoiding hallucinations. Stanford researchers tested them on over 200 legal questions.

    They did better than general-purpose GPT-4, but still produced incorrect or misgrounded answers on 17% to 33% of queries.

    RAG reduces hallucination. It doesn’t remove it. It has to be tested.

    Ask the roomGoogle’s glue answer accurately repeated what its source said. So was it a hallucination? Where exactly was the defect?
    Expected: not a hallucination in the classic sense: the answer was faithful to what was retrieved. The defect was upstream: retrieving and trusting a joke as a source. A faithfulness check alone would have passed it. You also need checks on what was retrieved.

    Part 1 · Context · RAG at a usage level

    A librarian and a writer

    Everyday version: you ask at a help desk. A librarian fetches the three most relevant pages from the shelves; a writer reads only those pages and answers you.

    Knowledge base

    The shelves: policies, FAQs, manuals, split into documents or chunks. Today: kb/lane_kb.txt, 15 documents, including an archived old policy and a marketing blog.

    Retriever

    The librarian: finds the top k documents for each question. Ours matches keywords; real systems use embeddings, but they fail in the same ways.

    Generator

    The writer: the LLM, told to answer only from what was retrieved. That’s Lane, as before.

    Ask the roomHarbour & Lane changes its returns policy tonight. What needs to change so a RAG assistant gives the new answer tomorrow?
    Expected: the document in the knowledge base, not the model and not the prompt. That’s RAG’s big advantage, and its big risk: if the old document isn’t removed or marked, the librarian may still fetch it.

    Part 1 · Context · Metric 1

    Context precision: are the right documents at the top?

    Everyday version: a search results page where the first three links are adverts. The right page is there, but on the wrong row.

    1. For each retrieved document, the judge asks: is it useful for reaching the reference answer?
    2. Useful documents ranked higher count more.
    3. Score near 1.0: the useful ones are on top. Low: noise is on top.

    R06 · “Will my Brera kettle work in New York?” · retrieved:

    1 · KB-91 blog: autumn kitchen buysnot useful
    2 · KB-08 using appliances abroaduseful
    3 · KB-03 warrantyuseful

    The useful ones are there, but noise is ranked first, so precision drops.

    Ask the roomWhy does it matter which position the useful document is in, if the writer reads all three anyway?
    Expected: models give more weight to what comes first, and real systems often cut off text when context is long. Noise at the top also wastes cost and invites the writer to use the wrong source, as Google did.

    Part 1 · Context · Metric 2

    Context recall: was everything needed actually fetched?

    Everyday version: an open-book exam where the librarian brought you chapter 4, but the answer is in chapter 7. No amount of good writing will save you.

    1. Split the reference answer into its statements.
    2. For each statement ask: can it be found in the retrieved documents?
    3. Score = statements covered ÷ all statements.

    R07 · “I ordered a mixer 2 hours ago by mistake. Can I cancel?”

    Retrieved: Support hours, Returns, Damaged items. The cancellation page (KB-10) is missing.

    Reference: “Orders can only be cancelled within 1 hour…” → that statement is nowhere in what was retrieved. Recall low.

    Why? The customer wrote “cancel”; the document says “Cancelling”. And “hours” matched the wrong page.

    Ask the roomContext recall needs a reference answer. Who should write it, and from which document?
    Expected: the tester with the business owner, from the current, correct document. If the reference is written from the archived policy, the test itself is wrong. References are part of the golden dataset from Day 7.

    Part 1 · Context · Answer correctness in a RAG setting

    Faithful is not the same as correct

    R02 · “Can I return an unused toaster after 20 days? Do I pay postage?”

    Retrieved first: KB-90, ARCHIVED 2023: “returns within 14 days, customer pays postage.”

    Reply: “Sorry, returns are only within 14 days.”

    Faithfulnesshigh: it matches a retrieved document
    Answer correctnesslow: the reference says 30 days, free label

    Faithfulness (Day 4) compares the reply with what was retrieved.

    Answer correctness compares the reply with the truth: your reference answer.

    In RAG you need both. A reply can be perfectly faithful to the wrong document. That’s the Google glue answer, and it’s Lane today.

    In the kit: rubrics/answer_correctness.txt, a G-Eval rubric (Day 6) comparing reply with reference.

    Ask the roomFaithfulness 0.95, answer correctness 0.20. Which layer do you suspect first, and what do you look at to confirm?
    Expected: retrieval. The writer stuck to what it was given, but what it was given was wrong. Confirm by looking at the retrieved document IDs in the report: is an archived or irrelevant document at the top?

    Part 1 · Context · What testers actually do

    Four habits for testing a RAG assistant

    1 · Write references from the right document

    Every RAG case gets a reference: line, the ideal answer. Precision, recall and correctness all depend on it.

    2 · Plant traps

    Include cases where an archived or look-alike document could win. That’s where RAG breaks in production.

    3 · Test the settings

    top_k (how many documents) is a trade-off: fewer means less noise but more misses, more means fewer misses but more noise. Measure it, don’t guess.

    4 · Treat documents like code

    A changed or deleted document is a release. Rerun the suite with the gate, the same as for a prompt change.

    Ask the roomIn your organisation, what changes more often: the chatbot’s prompt, or the documents it reads? Who tests those changes today?
    Expected: usually the documents: policies, FAQs and price lists change weekly, often edited by business teams with no testing at all. That’s the gap a RAG suite in CI closes.

    Part 3 · Wrap

    Broken-layer leaderboard, then three things to keep

    Two layers, separate scores

    Retrieval (precision, recall) and generation (faithfulness, correctness). One wrong answer, two possible owners.

    Faithful ≠ correct

    A reply can follow the wrong document perfectly. Correctness needs a reference written from the right document.

    Documents are releases

    Archive, reword or delete a document, and answers change. Same suite, same gate.
